Hearing Accessibility Guide

Making Information Available Beyond Sound

Videos, online lessons, and alerts often use sound to share important information. But not everyone can hear audio clearly, and some people browse with their sound turned off. Hearing accessibility provides other ways to follow along.

Add accurate captions

Captions show spoken words and meaningful sounds in videos. They help viewers follow the content without relying on audio.

Provide transcripts

A transcript gives users a written version of audio content that they can read at their own pace.

Use visual notifications

Important alerts should appear on the screen instead of being communicated through sound alone.

Offer accessible media controls

Give users a way to pause, replay, and control media whenever possible.

Make instructions available in text

Do not rely only on spoken directions. Written instructions help more people understand the next step.

Everyday Example

An online lesson with accurate captions allows students to follow the discussion even when they cannot hear the video.

Key Takeaway

Hearing accessibility makes information easier to follow by offering visual and written alternatives to sound.
