Cognitive Accessibility Guide

Keeping Online Experiences Clear and Simple

A website can be difficult to use when instructions are confusing, pages are crowded, or important buttons keep moving. Cognitive accessibility helps people who may have difficulties with attention, memory, reading, or processing information. Clear design also makes things easier for everyone.

Write in plain language

Use familiar words and short sentences. Explain technical terms when they are needed.

Organize content into sections

Headings, short paragraphs, and logical steps make information easier to follow.

Keep navigation consistent

Use familiar menus and keep important controls in predictable places.

Explain tasks clearly

Tell users what they need to do, one step at a time.

Make errors easy to fix

Instead of saying “Invalid input,” explain what went wrong and how to correct it.

Everyday Example

A form that says “Please enter a valid email address, such as name@example.com” is more helpful than one that only says “Error.”

Key Takeaway

Cognitive accessibility helps people understand information and complete tasks with less confusion.
